Cash home buyers can be a great option when you need to sell quickly or sell a house that needs work. But the industry has its share of bad actors, and sellers dealing with foreclosure, a death in the family or financial stress are often their targets. Here's how to tell a legitimate buyer from one to avoid.

## Red flag #1: They won't put the offer in writing

A verbal number means nothing. A legitimate buyer will give you a written offer that states the price, who pays which closing costs, the closing timeline and any conditions.

## Red flag #2: They pressure you to sign today

"This offer expires tonight" is a sales tactic, not a real deadline. A reputable buyer will give you time to think, talk it over with family and have an attorney review the contract.

## Red flag #3: They ask you for money

You shouldn't have to pay a buyer an application fee, "processing fee" or anything else to sell your house. Money should flow **to you** at closing, not from you before it.

## Red flag #4: They want to close with their own "closing person"

Legitimate sales close through a licensed, independent **title company or closing attorney** who holds the funds and records the deed properly. Be wary of anyone who wants you to sign a deed at your kitchen table or skip the closing process.

## Red flag #5: They ask you to sign over your deed

Be very cautious of anyone who asks you to transfer your deed before you've been paid, or who promises you can rent the house and "buy it back later." These arrangements are a common part of foreclosure rescue scams and can leave you without the house *and* still owing the mortgage.

## Red flag #6: The price drops right before closing

Some buyers make a high offer to get the house under contract, then find "surprises" that slash the price once you're committed and out of time. Ask up front how firm the offer is, what could change it, and how long the inspection period lasts.

## Red flag #7: A long inspection period and tiny earnest money

A contract with a 30- or 60-day inspection period and $10 of earnest money lets a buyer tie up your house while they shop it around, then walk away for free. Reasonable inspection periods and meaningful earnest money show commitment.

## Red flag #8: They're vague about who's actually buying

Some companies ("wholesalers") sign a contract with you and then assign it to a different investor who actually closes. That can be perfectly legitimate, and it's how many cash sales happen. But **you deserve to know up front,** and a growing number of states require it to be disclosed. Ask directly. (Here's [how wholesaling works](/blog/what-is-a-real-estate-wholesaler).)

## Red flag #9: No real presence or track record

Look for a verifiable local phone number, a physical or service-area presence, a real website with real people, and reviews on independent sites like Google. Search the company name plus "reviews" and "complaints."

## Questions every seller should ask

1. Will you put the offer in writing, including who pays closing costs?
2. Is the price firm? What could change it?
3. How long is your inspection or due diligence period?
4. How much earnest money will you deposit, and where will it be held?
5. Which title company or closing attorney will handle the closing?
6. Will you buy the house yourself, or might you assign the contract?
7. Can I have my attorney review the contract before I sign?
