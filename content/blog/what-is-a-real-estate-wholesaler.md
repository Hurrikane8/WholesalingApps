If you've received a "we buy houses" flyer, text or knock on the door, there's a good chance it came from a real estate wholesaler. Wholesaling is common, often misunderstood, and worth understanding before you sign anything. Here's an honest look at how it works and what it means for you as a seller.

## What a wholesaler does

A wholesaler finds homeowners who want to sell, usually quickly or as-is, and signs a contract to buy their property. Then, instead of always completing the purchase themselves, the wholesaler may:

- **Assign the contract** to another investor (the "end buyer"), who pays the wholesaler a fee for it, or
- **Complete the purchase** and resell the property, sometimes on the same day

The end buyer is typically a local investor who plans to renovate and resell the home, or keep it as a rental.

## How an assignment works, step by step

1. You and the wholesaler sign a purchase contract at an agreed price.
2. The wholesaler finds an investor willing to buy on the same terms.
3. The contract is assigned to that investor, who pays the wholesaler an assignment fee.
4. The investor completes the purchase with you through the lawyers. You receive the contract price you agreed to.

Your price doesn't change because of the assignment. The fee is paid by the end buyer, on top of your price.

## Why sellers work with wholesalers

- **Speed and simplicity.** Wholesalers work with networks of cash investors who can close quickly.
- **As-is sales.** Homes needing major work, or with tenants, arrears or messy situations, can still find a buyer.
- **No commission or showings.**

## The risks, and how to avoid them

Wholesaling works when it's done transparently. Problems come up when it isn't. Watch for:

- **Contracts they can't complete.** If no investor wants the deal at that price, some wholesalers cancel or pressure you to lower it. Ask what happens if they can't find a buyer.
- **Long conditions and small deposits,** which let a buyer tie up your home at little cost to them.
- **A lack of disclosure.** You should know up front if your contract might be assigned.

## The rules in Alberta

In Alberta, trading in real estate on behalf of others is regulated by the **Real Estate Council of Alberta (RECA)**, and many activities require a licence. Buying property for yourself as a principal is different from representing other people, and where a wholesaler's activities fall matters. If you have questions about how a particular buyer operates, ask them directly, check RECA's public resources, and have a real estate lawyer review the contract before you sign.

## Questions to ask any wholesaler

1. Do you intend to complete the purchase yourself, or assign the contract?
2. Who is the likely end buyer, and can they show proof of funds?
3. How much is the deposit, whose trust account will hold it, and when is it paid?
4. What conditions are in the contract, and when do they come off?
5. Which lawyer will act for you?
6. Are you licensed with RECA, and if not, how does your business work within Alberta's rules?
