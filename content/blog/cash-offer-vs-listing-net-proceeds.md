When you're deciding how to sell a house, it's natural to focus on the price. But the number that actually matters is your **net proceeds**: what you walk away with after every cost, fee and month of waiting. Two offers that look far apart on paper can end up surprisingly close once you do the math.

Here's a simple, honest framework you can use to compare a cash offer with listing through an agent, whether you sell to us or not.

## Step 1: Start with a realistic sale price

For listing, the question isn't "what did the house down the street sell for?" It's **"what would my house sell for in its current condition, or after the repairs I'm actually willing to make?"**

- Ask a local agent for a comparative market analysis (CMA) and ask them to price it **as-is** and **after repairs**.
- Look at recent sales of similar homes (same size, age and condition) within the last few months.
- Be realistic about condition. Buyers using mortgages, and their appraisers and inspectors, will notice roof, foundation, electrical and plumbing issues.

For a cash offer, the price is simply the number in writing. Make sure you know whether it's firm or "subject to" an inspection.

## Step 2: Subtract the costs of selling

Listing a home comes with costs that a direct sale usually doesn't:

- **Agent commissions.** Historically, total commissions have commonly been around 5–6% of the sale price. Since 2024 rule changes, how buyer-agent compensation is negotiated has changed, and commissions have always been negotiable, so ask your agent for a written estimate.
- **Seller closing costs.** Depending on your state, these can include title or attorney fees, transfer taxes, recording fees and prorated property taxes. A rough planning range is 1–3% of the price.
- **Buyer concessions.** Buyers often ask for help with their closing costs or credits for repairs found during inspection.

## Step 3: Subtract repairs and preparation

Most listed homes need some work to compete:

- Pre-listing repairs (roof, HVAC, plumbing, electrical)
- Paint, flooring, landscaping and deep cleaning
- Staging and professional photos
- Repairs requested after the buyer's inspection

If your house needs significant work, this line can be the largest one on the sheet. It also has to be paid **up front**, before you sell.

## Step 4: Add up holding costs

Every month the house is on the market (plus the weeks of repairs before and the 30–60 days to close after an accepted offer), you keep paying for it:

- Mortgage payments (or at least the interest)
- Property taxes and homeowner's insurance, which can cost more if the home is vacant
- Utilities, lawn care, HOA dues and security

Multiply your monthly total by a realistic number of months. For many homes, that's 3–6 months from the start of prep to closing.

## Step 5: Weigh time, certainty and stress

Some costs don't show up on a spreadsheet:

- **Deals fall through.** Financing, appraisal and inspection problems can send you back to square one.
- **Showings disrupt your life,** especially with kids, pets, tenants or a busy schedule.
- **Deadlines matter.** If you're facing foreclosure, a relocation date or a divorce settlement, speed has real value.

## Questions to ask any cash buyer

Not all cash offers are equal. Before you sign with anyone, ask:

1. **Can you show proof of funds?**
2. **Who will actually buy the house?** Will you close yourself, or might you assign the contract to another buyer? (We always tell you in writing.)
3. **How much earnest money will you deposit, and with whom?**
4. **Is there an inspection period, and what happens if you find something?**
5. **Which closing costs do you pay?**
6. **Which title company or closing attorney will handle the closing?**

A reputable buyer will answer every one of these clearly and in writing.

## The bottom line

If your house is in great shape and you have time, listing will often get you the most money. If it needs work, time is short, or you value certainty, a cash sale can put a similar amount in your pocket much sooner, with far less hassle. The only way to know is to compare real numbers side by side.
