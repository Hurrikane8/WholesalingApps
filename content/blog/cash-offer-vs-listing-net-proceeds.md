When you're deciding how to sell a home, it's natural to focus on the price. But the number that actually matters is your **net proceeds**: what you walk away with after every cost, fee and month of waiting. Two options that look far apart on paper can end up closer once you do the math.

Here's a simple, honest way to compare a cash offer with listing through a realtor in Alberta, whether you sell to us or not.

## Step 1: Start with a realistic sale price

For listing, the question isn't "what did the one down the street sell for?" It's **"what would my home sell for in its current condition, or after the repairs I'm actually willing to make?"**

- Ask a realtor for a comparative market analysis, priced both **as-is** and **after repairs**.
- Look at recent sales of similar homes (same size, age, style and condition) in the last few months.
- For condos, compare within the same complex or similar ones, because condo fees and the complex's condition affect price a lot.
- Be realistic about condition. Buyers, their lenders and their inspectors will notice roof, foundation, plumbing and electrical issues.

For a cash offer, the price is simply the number in writing. Make sure you know whether it's firm or subject to conditions.

## Step 2: Subtract the costs of selling

Listing comes with costs that a direct sale usually doesn't:

- **Commission.** In Alberta, commissions are often calculated on a tiered formula, for example 7% on the first $100,000 of the price and 3% on the rest, **plus GST**. On a $400,000 sale that works out to $16,000 plus $800 GST. Commissions are negotiable, so ask for a written estimate.
- **Legal fees and disbursements.** Your real estate lawyer's fees, plus the cost of discharging your mortgage.
- **Condo document fees**, if you're selling a condo and need to order documents from the management company.
- **Buyer credits or price reductions** after the inspection.

Alberta has no land transfer tax, which keeps closing costs lower than in some provinces. Sellers mainly pay legal fees and discharge costs.

## Step 3: Subtract repairs and preparation

Most listed homes need some work to compete:

- Pre-listing repairs (roof, furnace, plumbing, electrical)
- Paint, flooring, fixtures and a deep clean
- Staging and professional photos
- Repairs or credits requested after the buyer's inspection

If your home needs significant work, this can be the biggest line on the sheet, and it has to be paid **up front**, before you sell.

## Step 4: Add up holding costs

Every month the home is on the market, plus the weeks of repairs before and the 30–60 days to possession after, you keep paying for it:

- Mortgage payments
- Property taxes and home insurance (which can cost more if the home is vacant)
- Condo fees, if you have them
- Utilities, snow removal and upkeep

Multiply your monthly total by a realistic number of months. For many homes, it's three to six months from the start of prep to closing.

## Step 5: Don't forget your mortgage

Your mortgage gets paid out when you sell either way, but check whether you'll owe a **prepayment penalty** for breaking a fixed-rate mortgage early. It can be significant. It applies whether you list or sell for cash, so it doesn't change the comparison, but it does change what you walk away with. Your lender can give you a payout statement.

## Step 6: Weigh time, certainty and stress

Some costs don't show up on a spreadsheet:

- **Deals collapse.** Financing, inspection and condo document conditions can send you back to square one.
- **Showings disrupt your life,** especially with kids, pets, tenants or shift work.
- **Deadlines matter.** If you're facing foreclosure, a move or a separation agreement, speed has real value.

## Questions to ask any cash buyer

Not all cash offers are equal. Before you sign with anyone, ask:

1. **Can you show proof of funds?**
2. **Who will actually buy the home?** Will you close yourself, or might you assign the contract? (We always tell you in writing.)
3. **How much is the deposit, and whose trust account will hold it?**
4. **What conditions are in the offer, and when do they come off?**
5. **Which costs do you cover?**
6. **Which lawyer will act for you?**

A reputable buyer will answer every one of these clearly and in writing.

## The bottom line

If your home is in great shape and you have time, listing will often get you the most money. If it needs work, time is short, or you value certainty, a cash sale trades some of the price for speed and simplicity. The only way to know is to compare real numbers side by side.
