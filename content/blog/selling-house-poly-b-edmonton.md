If your house was built or re-plumbed in the 1980s or '90s, there's a chance its water lines are Poly-B: grey (sometimes blue or black) plastic pipe. It's one of the first things a buyer's inspector looks for, and one of the first questions an insurer asks. Here's why, and what your options are when you sell.

*General information. Talk to a licensed plumber about your pipes, and to your insurer about your policy.*

## What Poly-B is

Polybutylene, or Poly-B, is a flexible plastic pipe that was installed for water supply lines in many Canadian homes from the late 1970s to the late 1990s. <!-- VERIFY: installation years and failure causes, against a primary or industry source --> Over time, some Poly-B systems, and especially their fittings, have been prone to leaks.

A plumber or home inspector can usually tell you whether a house has it by looking where the water line enters the house, at the hot water tank, and under sinks.

## Why insurers and lenders ask about it

<!-- VERIFY: current insurer practices with at least one Alberta broker; e.g. https://www.westlandinsurance.ca/news/has-your-broker-asked-if-you-have-polybutylene-poly-b-plumbing/ -->
Because of the leak risk, many home insurers ask whether a house has Poly-B. Depending on the insurer, the answer can mean a higher premium, limits on water damage coverage, a deadline to replace it, or no coverage at all. A buyer who can't get insurance can't get a mortgage, so Poly-B becomes the buyer's problem, and then yours.

Ask your own insurer what applies to your policy; practices differ and they change.

## Your options when you sell

1. **Replace it before you list.** A plumber re-pipes the house, usually in PEX, and patches the openings. You sell a house without the question mark, but you pay up front and live through the work. Get a written quote from a licensed plumber so you know the real cost for your house.
2. **Credit the buyer.** List the house with Poly-B and offer a credit or a price reduction based on a written replacement quote. The buyer's insurer and lender still need to be satisfied, so this works best with a quote in hand from the start.
3. **Sell as-is.** Sell to a buyer who prices the replacement into the offer. You don't pay for the work or manage it, and the price reflects the cost.

Which is best depends on the quote, your cash, the rest of the house, and how much time you have. If the house needs other big work too, as-is often makes more sense; if Poly-B is the only issue, replacing it or crediting the buyer may net you more.

## Don't hide it

Selling as-is doesn't mean you can hide known problems. If you know the house has Poly-B, or has had leaks, say so. The inspector will likely find it anyway.

## The bottom line

Poly-B turns up in many homes of that era, and it can be fixed. It affects insurance, financing and price, so get a written quote and compare your options with real numbers. The [cash vs. listing calculator](/cash-offer-vs-realtor#calculator) helps.
