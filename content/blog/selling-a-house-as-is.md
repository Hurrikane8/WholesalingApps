"As-is" is one of the most used, and most misunderstood, phrases in real estate. Here's what it actually means when you sell a home as-is in Alberta, what it doesn't mean, and how to decide whether it's the right move.

## What "as-is" means

Selling as-is means you're selling the home **in its current condition, without making repairs or giving repair credits.** The buyer accepts the property with its flaws, and the price reflects that.

In practice, an as-is sale usually means:

- No repairs before listing or before closing
- No negotiating a repair list after the buyer's inspection
- The buyer takes care of whatever needs fixing after possession

## What "as-is" does *not* mean

- **It doesn't mean you can hide known problems.** Alberta real estate law leans toward "buyer beware", but sellers generally can't conceal serious known defects or misrepresent the property. If you know about a leaky basement, a cracked foundation or a past sewer backup, say so. Your lawyer or realtor can tell you exactly what you need to disclose.
- **It doesn't always mean no inspection.** Many as-is buyers still inspect. They just won't ask you to fix things, though some may try to renegotiate the price or walk away while their conditions are open.
- **It doesn't automatically mean a lowball offer.** It means the price accounts for the condition. How fair that price is depends on the buyer.

## Two ways to sell as-is

### 1. List it as-is with a realtor

You can list a home on the market and make clear it's being sold as-is. This can work well when it needs mostly cosmetic work and demand is strong. Keep in mind:

- Buyers who need a mortgage may struggle to finance, or insure, a home with serious issues
- Buyers may still ask for price cuts after their inspection
- You'll still have showings, commission plus GST, and typically 30–60 days to possession after an accepted offer

### 2. Sell as-is to a cash buyer

Investors and home-buying companies buy homes as-is for cash. The trade-off is usually a lower price in exchange for:

- No repairs, showings or commission
- No financing condition
- A faster, more certain closing on your chosen date

## Is an as-is sale right for you?

An as-is sale often makes sense when:

- The home needs repairs you can't afford or don't want to manage
- You need to sell quickly (foreclosure, a move, a separation, an estate)
- Lenders or insurers are likely to balk at the home's condition (think Poly-B plumbing, knob-and-tube wiring or foundation issues)
- You've inherited a property and want a simple exit

Repairing first may be better when the work is cosmetic, you have the budget and time, and updated homes in your area sell at a real premium.

## Tips for a smooth as-is sale

1. **Know your numbers.** Get a sense of what the home would sell for as-is and after repairs, and what the repairs would cost.
2. **Be upfront about what you know.** It protects you and speeds up the deal.
3. **Read the contract.** Look at the conditions, the deposit, and when the conditions come off.
