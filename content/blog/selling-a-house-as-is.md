"As-is" is one of the most used, and most misunderstood, phrases in real estate. Here's what it actually means when you sell a house as-is, what it doesn't mean, and how to decide whether it's the right move.

## What "as-is" means

Selling a house as-is means you're selling it **in its current condition, without making repairs or offering repair credits.** The buyer accepts the property with its flaws, and the price reflects that.

In practice, an as-is sale usually means:

- No repairs before listing or before closing
- No negotiating a repair list after the buyer's inspection
- The buyer is responsible for fixing whatever needs fixing after closing

## What "as-is" does *not* mean

- **It doesn't mean you can hide known problems.** Many states require sellers to disclose known material defects (like a leaky roof, foundation issues or past flooding), even in an as-is sale. Failing to disclose can expose you to legal claims later.
- **It doesn't always mean no inspection.** Many as-is buyers still inspect the house. They just won't ask you to fix things (though some may renegotiate the price or walk away within their inspection period).
- **It doesn't mean you'll get a lowball offer by definition.** It means the price accounts for the condition. How fair that price is depends on the buyer.

## Two ways to sell as-is

### 1. List it as-is with an agent

You can list a house on the open market and state that it's being sold as-is. This can work well when the house needs mostly cosmetic work and there's strong buyer demand. Keep in mind:

- Buyers using FHA, VA and some conventional loans may not be able to finance a house with significant condition issues
- Buyers may still ask for price reductions after their inspection
- You'll still have showings, commissions and a typical 30- to 60-day closing after accepting an offer

### 2. Sell as-is to a cash buyer

Direct buyers such as investors and home-buying companies purchase houses as-is for cash. The trade-off is usually a lower price in exchange for:

- No repairs, showings or commissions
- No financing contingencies
- A faster, more certain closing on your chosen date

## Is an as-is sale right for you?

An as-is sale often makes sense when:

- The house needs repairs you can't afford or don't want to manage
- You need to sell quickly (foreclosure, relocation, divorce, estate)
- The house wouldn't qualify for most mortgages in its current condition
- You've inherited a property and want a simple exit

Repairing first may be better when the work is cosmetic, you have the budget and time, and updated homes in your area sell at a big premium.

## Tips for a smooth as-is sale

1. **Know your numbers.** Get an idea of what the house would sell for as-is and after repairs, and what the repairs would cost.
2. **Disclose honestly.** It protects you and speeds up the deal.
3. **Read the contract.** Look at the inspection period, earnest money and cancellation terms.
