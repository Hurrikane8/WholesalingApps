## What the title company does in the meantime

Once you sign, the title company or closing attorney:

- Searches public records for liens, judgments and ownership issues
- Requests payoff statements from your mortgage servicer and any other lienholders
- Prepares the deed and closing documents
- Coordinates signing, either in person, with a mobile notary or remotely where allowed

This is usually what sets the pace, not the buyer.

## What can slow a cash sale down

- **Title issues:** unreleased old mortgages, judgments, missing heirs or errors in the public record
- **Probate:** if the house is inherited, the executor may need court authority before closing
- **Multiple owners:** everyone on title has to sign
- **Liens that need negotiating,** such as some code enforcement liens or judgments
- **Lender delays** in providing payoff statements (or approving a short sale)

None of these are deal-breakers. They just take time to resolve, and an experienced buyer and title company will help work through them.

## What can speed it up

- Having your mortgage statement, ID and any HOA details handy
- Knowing of any liens, judgments or title issues up front
- Making sure everyone on title is available to sign
- Responding quickly to the title company's requests

## Cash sale vs. listing: the timeline

A traditional listing usually involves:

1. **Preparing the house:** repairs, cleaning, staging and photos (often a few weeks or more)
2. **Time on the market** until an acceptable offer comes in (varies widely by market and price)
3. **Escrow period** of commonly 30 to 60 days while the buyer's loan, appraisal and inspection are completed

All told, three to six months from decision to closing is common, and longer if a deal falls through and you have to start over. A cash sale skips the preparation, the market time and the financing wait.

## The bottom line

A cash sale can realistically close in one to three weeks, and you can choose a later date if you need more time. The biggest variable is the title, not the buyer.
