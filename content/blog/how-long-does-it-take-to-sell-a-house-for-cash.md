A quick sale is one of the main reasons people sell for cash. But a buyer's promise of a one-week sale doesn't tell the whole story. Here's what happens between the first call and closing in Alberta, what can speed it up or slow it down, and how it compares with listing.

## The steps in a cash sale

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| First call or form | A few questions about the place and what you need |
| The visit | One visit, in person or by video |
| Written offer | With me, sent {{offerTimingPhrase}} |
| Purchase contract | Signed when you're ready, with your lawyer's review |
| The lawyers' work | Title search, payout statements, documents |
| Closing | On the date in the contract; with me, {{closingPhrase}} |

The closing date is set in the contract, so you can choose one that gives you time to move.

## What the lawyers do in the meantime

Once you sign, your lawyer and the buyer's lawyer:

- search the title at Land Titles for mortgages, liens, caveats and writs;
- get payout statements from your lender and anyone else registered on title;
- prepare the transfer and closing documents, and a statement of adjustments for property taxes, condo fees and rent;
- arrange for you to sign, in person or with a lawyer or notary where you are.

This is usually what sets the pace, not the buyer. Ask your lawyer how long they need for your file.

## What can slow a cash sale down

- **Title issues:** old mortgages that were never discharged, writs, caveats or errors on title.
- **Estates:** the personal representative may need a grant of probate before closing.
- **Multiple owners:** everyone on title, plus any spouse whose consent is required, has to sign.
- **Condo documents:** getting documents or an estoppel certificate from the management company.
- **Registrations that need negotiating,** such as some writs or builders' liens.
- **Lender delays** in providing payout statements.

None of these has to end a sale. They take time, and good lawyers work through them.

## What can speed it up

- Having your mortgage statement, ID and any condo documents ready.
- Telling the buyer about any arrears, liens or title issues up front.
- Making sure everyone who needs to sign is available.
- Picking your lawyer early and answering their requests quickly.

## Cash sale vs. listing: the timeline

A traditional listing usually involves:

1. **Getting the home ready:** repairs, cleaning, staging and photos.
2. **Time on the market** until an acceptable offer comes in, which varies widely by area, price and season.
3. **Conditions and possession:** a period for the buyer's financing, inspection and condo document conditions, then a possession date that's often a month or more away.

Add it up, and a listing often takes months from decision to closing, longer if a deal collapses and you have to start over. A cash sale skips the preparation, the market time and the financing condition. Whether that's worth the lower price depends on your home and your deadline; the [cash vs. listing calculator](/cash-offer-vs-realtor#calculator) helps you weigh it.

## The bottom line

With a cash sale, the biggest variable is usually the title and the paperwork, not the buyer, and you can choose a later date if you need more time.
