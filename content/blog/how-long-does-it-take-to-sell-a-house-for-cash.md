One of the biggest reasons people sell for cash is speed. But a buyer's promise of a one-week sale doesn't tell the whole story. Here's a realistic timeline for a cash sale in Alberta, what can speed it up or slow it down, and how it compares with listing.

## What the lawyers do in the meantime

Once you sign, your lawyer and the buyer's lawyer:

- Search the title at Land Titles for mortgages, liens, caveats and writs
- Get payout statements from your lender and anyone else registered on title
- Prepare the transfer and closing documents, and a statement of adjustments for property taxes, condo fees and rent
- Arrange for you to sign, in person or with a lawyer or notary where you are

This is usually what sets the pace, not the buyer.

## What can slow a cash sale down

- **Title issues:** old mortgages that were never discharged, writs, caveats or errors on title
- **Estates:** the personal representative may need a grant of probate before closing
- **Multiple owners:** everyone on title, plus any spouse whose consent is required, has to sign
- **Condo documents:** getting documents or an estoppel certificate from the management company
- **Registrations that need negotiating,** such as some writs or builders' liens
- **Lender delays** in providing payout statements

None of these are deal-breakers. They just take time, and an experienced buyer and good lawyers will help work through them.

## What can speed it up

- Having your mortgage statement, ID and any condo documents ready
- Telling the buyer about any arrears, liens or title issues up front
- Making sure everyone who needs to sign is available
- Picking your lawyer early and responding quickly to their requests

## Cash sale vs. listing: the timeline

A traditional listing usually involves:

1. **Getting the home ready:** repairs, cleaning, staging and photos (often a few weeks or more)
2. **Time on the market** until an acceptable offer comes in (varies widely by area, price and season)
3. **Conditions and possession:** usually a week or two for financing, inspection and condo document conditions, then commonly 30–60 days to possession

From decision to closing, three to six months is common, and longer if a deal collapses and you have to start over. A cash sale skips the preparation, the market time and the financing wait.

## The bottom line

A cash sale in Alberta can realistically close in one to three weeks, and you can choose a later date if you need more time. The biggest variable is usually the title and the paperwork, not the buyer.
