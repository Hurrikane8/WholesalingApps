A half duplex is two homes under one roof: one side is yours, the other side is your neighbour's. That makes it a good-value home, and it also raises questions a buyer of a detached house never has to ask. Here's how selling one works, and when a direct sale fits.

## Shared walls and roofs

The wall between the two sides, and often the roof, the foundation and the driveway, are shared. How that's handled depends on the title:

- **Separate titles.** Each side is its own property. There may be a party wall agreement or an easement on title that says who maintains what and how costs are split.
- **A two-unit condo.** Some half duplexes are set up as a small condominium with bylaws. The bylaws, not a handshake, decide how shared repairs are paid for.

Buyers with a mortgage, and their lenders, look at these arrangements closely. A roof that's due on one side, or no written agreement at all, can slow a sale down. I read what's on title, price in the shared repairs, and show you how.

## Basement suites

Many half duplexes have a basement suite. Tell me whether it has permits, whether it's rented, and what the tenant pays. A suite without permits usually doesn't stop a sale, but it changes what the next owner can do with it, so it's part of the math, not a reason to walk away.

## Selling one side

You can usually sell your side without your neighbour's agreement if it has its own title. In a two-unit condo, the bylaws may set out notice or other steps; your lawyer will confirm what applies. Either way, I'll work around your neighbour for the visit, and nothing about your sale needs to involve them.

If there are tenants upstairs or down, the tenancies generally carry over to the buyer. I'll need the leases and the rent details.

## When a direct sale fits

If your side is in good shape and you have time, listing will probably net you more, and I'll say so. A direct sale can make more sense when the shared roof or wall needs work, a suite needs sorting out, tenants are in place, or you need a firm date.
