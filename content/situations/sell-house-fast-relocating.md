A new job, a family need or a fresh start can come with a hard deadline. Selling a house at the same time, especially from another city or province, can mean months of paying for two homes, arranging repairs from a distance and showings you can't attend.

## What a slow sale costs when you're moving

When you move before your home sells, you're often paying for two places at once:

- **mortgage, property taxes, condo fees and insurance** on the old home, plus rent or a new mortgage;
- **utilities, snow removal, lawn care and upkeep** on a place you're not living in;
- **vacancy risks** in an Edmonton winter: a furnace that quits, frozen pipes nobody notices, or insurance conditions that require regular checks on an empty home;
- **trips back** for repairs, inspections or signing.

Even a few extra months adds up. The [cash vs. listing calculator](/cash-offer-vs-realtor#calculator) lets you put your own carrying costs into the comparison.

## Your options

- **List with a realtor and manage the sale from afar.** Works well if the home shows well and you can carry both payments for a while.
- **Rent it out.** Possible, but being a long-distance landlord usually means paying a property manager and taking on vacancy and repair risk.
- **Use your employer's relocation program,** if there is one. Compare the terms carefully.
- **Sell as-is to a direct buyer.** A known price and a known date, with no showings and no repairs.

## How a direct sale fits a move

- **You pick the closing date.** Close before you leave so the money is ready for your next home, or after you've moved.
- **Sell from anywhere.** I can see the house by video or with a lockbox, and your lawyer can arrange signing wherever you are.
- **Skip the prep.** No repairs, painting, staging or keeping the place spotless for showings.
