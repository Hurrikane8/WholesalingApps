A new job, a family need or a fresh start can come with a hard deadline. Trying to sell a house at the same time, especially from another city, can mean months of double housing payments, remote repair coordination and showings you can't attend.

## The real cost of a slow sale when you're moving

When you relocate before your house sells, you're often paying for two homes at once:

- **Mortgage, taxes and insurance** on the old house, plus rent or a new mortgage
- **Utilities, lawn care and upkeep** on a house you're not living in
- **Vacancy risks** such as break-ins, leaks that go unnoticed, and insurance coverage that can shrink once a home sits empty
- **Travel back and forth** for repairs, inspections or closing

Even a few extra months can add up to thousands of dollars.

## Your options

- **List with an agent and manage the sale remotely.** It works well if the house shows well and you can carry both payments for a while.
- **Rent it out.** Possible, but being a long-distance landlord usually means paying a property manager and taking on vacancy and repair risk.
- **Use your employer's relocation program,** if one is available. Compare its terms carefully.
- **Sell as-is for cash.** You get a known price and a known date, with no showings and no repairs.

## How a cash sale helps you relocate

- **Pick your closing date.** Close before you leave so the money is available for your next home, or close after you've moved.
- **Sell from anywhere.** Walkthroughs can happen by video or with a lockbox, and closings can often be signed remotely.
- **Skip the prep.** No repairs, no painting, no staging, and no keeping the house spotless for showings.
- **Leave what won't fit.** Take what you need; we'll handle the rest.
