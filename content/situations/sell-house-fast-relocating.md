A new job, a family need or a fresh start can come with a hard deadline. Trying to sell a house at the same time, especially from another city or province, can mean months of paying for two homes, coordinating repairs from a distance and showings you can't attend.

## The real cost of a slow sale when you're moving

When you move before your home sells, you're often paying for two places at once:

- **Mortgage, property taxes, condo fees and insurance** on the old home, plus rent or a new mortgage
- **Utilities, snow removal, lawn care and upkeep** on a place you're not living in
- **Vacancy risks** in an Edmonton winter, like a furnace that quits, frozen pipes that go unnoticed, or insurance conditions that require regular checks on an empty home
- **Trips back** for repairs, inspections or signing

Even a few extra months can add up to thousands of dollars.

## Your options

- **List with a realtor and manage the sale from afar.** Works well if the home shows well and you can carry both payments for a while.
- **Rent it out.** Possible, but being a long-distance landlord usually means paying a property manager and taking on vacancy and repair risk.
- **Use your employer's relocation program,** if there is one. Compare the terms carefully.
- **Sell as-is for cash.** A known price and a known date, with no showings and no repairs.

## How a cash sale helps you relocate

- **Pick your closing date.** Close before you leave so the money is ready for your next home, or close after you've moved.
- **Sell from anywhere.** Walkthroughs can happen by video or with a lockbox, and your lawyer can arrange signing wherever you are.
- **Skip the prep.** No repairs, painting, staging or keeping the place spotless for showings.
- **Leave what won't fit.** Take what you need; we'll handle the rest.
