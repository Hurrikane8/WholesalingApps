An empty house can feel like a problem you can deal with later. But vacant properties quietly cost money every month, and they tend to attract problems the longer they sit.

## The hidden costs of a vacant house

- **Carrying costs.** Property taxes, insurance, utilities, lawn care and HOA dues keep coming due, with no one living there to benefit.
- **Insurance gaps.** Many standard homeowner's policies limit coverage once a home has been vacant for a period of time, often 30 to 60 days. Vacant-home policies cost more.
- **Undetected damage.** A slow leak, a failed sump pump or frozen pipes can do serious damage before anyone notices.
- **Break-ins, vandalism and squatters.** Empty houses are easy targets.
- **Code enforcement.** Overgrown yards, boarded windows and trash can bring notices, fines and liens from the city.

## Why houses end up vacant

We buy vacant houses for all kinds of reasons: an inherited home no one wants to live in, a former rental between tenants, a house left behind after a move or a job transfer, or a property that simply needs more work than the owner wants to take on.

## Your options

- **Rent it out.** You'll likely need repairs first, plus a property manager if you're not local.
- **Renovate and list it.** It can bring top dollar, but it takes cash, time and contractor management.
- **Sell it as-is.** It stops the carrying costs and the risk right away.
