An empty house can feel like a problem you'll deal with later. But vacant homes quietly cost money every month, and in Edmonton they carry a risk many places don't: winter.

## What a vacant house costs

- **Carrying costs.** Property taxes, insurance, heat, power, snow removal and condo fees keep coming due, with no one living there.
- **Insurance conditions.** Many home insurance policies limit coverage once a home has been vacant for a while, or require someone to check on it regularly, especially in the heating season. Vacant-home coverage costs more.
- **Freeze-ups.** A furnace that quits during a cold snap can mean burst pipes and serious water damage before anyone notices.
- **Break-ins, vandalism and squatters.** Empty houses are easy targets.
- **Bylaw enforcement.** Long grass, snow-covered walks, boarded windows and garbage can bring notices and orders from the city.

## How houses end up empty

An inherited house no one wants to live in. A rental between tenants. A home left behind after a move, or one that needs more work than the owner wants to take on. Whatever the reason, the monthly costs and the winter risk are the same.

## Your options

- **Rent it out.** You'll likely need repairs first, plus a property manager if you're not local.
- **Renovate and list it.** It can bring the highest price, but it takes cash, time and contractors. If that's the better route, I'll say so.
- **Sell it as-is.** It stops the carrying costs and the risk on the closing date.
