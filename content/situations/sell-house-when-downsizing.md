After years, or decades, in the same house, the stairs get harder, the yard gets bigger and the empty bedrooms stay empty. Whether you're moving somewhere smaller, closer to family or into assisted living, selling the family home can be emotional and exhausting. The process shouldn't add to it.

## Why downsizing sales get complicated

- **A lifetime of belongings** to sort, donate or sell
- **Updates buyers expect,** like newer kitchens, bathrooms, flooring and systems, that older homes often need before listing
- **Showings and open houses** that disrupt daily routines, which is especially hard with health or mobility challenges
- **Coordinating two moves,** getting the timing right between selling and moving into the next place
- **Family decision-making,** especially when adult children are helping from a distance

## Your options

- **List with an agent.** It often brings the highest price for a well-maintained, updated home, if you have the time and energy for preparation and showings.
- **Work with a senior move manager** to handle sorting, packing and the move, then list the house.
- **Sell it as-is for cash** to skip the preparation and showings entirely.

## How a direct sale makes downsizing easier

- **Leave what you don't need.** Take the treasures; we'll handle everything else after closing.

## Selling on behalf of a parent

If you're helping a parent sell, make sure the right person has the authority to sign. That's usually the owner, an agent under a durable power of attorney that covers real estate, or a court-appointed guardian or conservator. The title company or closing attorney will confirm the paperwork before closing.
