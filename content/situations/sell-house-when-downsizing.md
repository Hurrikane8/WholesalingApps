After years, or decades, in the same house, the stairs get harder, the yard and the snow get bigger, and the empty bedrooms stay empty. Whether you're moving somewhere smaller, closer to family or into seniors' housing, selling the family home can be emotional and tiring. The process shouldn't add to it.

## Why downsizing sales get complicated

- **A lifetime of belongings** to sort, donate or sell.
- **Updates buyers expect,** like newer kitchens, bathrooms, flooring, windows and furnaces, that older homes often need before listing.
- **Showings and open houses** that disrupt daily routines, which is especially hard with health or mobility challenges.
- **Timing two moves,** so the sale lines up with a suite or a room becoming available.
- **Family decisions,** especially when adult children are helping from out of town.

## Your options

- **List with a realtor.** Often the highest price for a well-kept, updated home, if you have the time and energy for preparation and showings. If that's you, I'll say so.
- **Work with a seniors' move manager** to handle sorting, packing and the move, then list.
- **Sell as-is to a direct buyer** and skip the preparation and showings.

## How a direct sale can make downsizing easier

- **One visit, not dozens,** at a time that suits you.
- **No repairs or updates.** The condition sets the price.
- **Belongings.** Take the treasures. What stays behind is agreed in the contract.
- **Your timeline.** We close {{closingPhrase}}, so you can wait until you're settled in your new place.
- **Family included.** Adult children, other family members or advisors are welcome in every conversation.

## Selling on behalf of a parent

If you're helping a parent sell, make sure the right person has authority to sign. That's usually the owner, an attorney acting under an enduring power of attorney, or a court-appointed trustee. The lawyers will confirm the paperwork before closing.
