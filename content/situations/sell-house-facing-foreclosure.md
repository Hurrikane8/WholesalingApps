Falling behind on your mortgage is stressful, and foreclosure notices are designed to get your attention. The most important thing to know is that **you usually have more options than it feels like, especially if you act early.** Every week you wait, some of those options disappear.

## How foreclosure usually unfolds

Every state handles foreclosure a little differently. In some it runs through the courts and takes many months; in others it can move much faster. But the general pattern looks like this:

1. **Missed payments.** Your servicer charges late fees and starts contacting you.
2. **Pre-foreclosure.** Under federal rules, mortgage servicers generally can't make the first foreclosure filing until you're more than 120 days behind, and they have to tell you about options to avoid foreclosure along the way.
3. **Foreclosure notice.** You'll receive formal notice of the foreclosure and, eventually, a sale date.
4. **Foreclosure sale.** If the loan isn't brought current or paid off, the property is sold, often at auction.

Until that sale happens, you normally still own your home, and you have the right to sell it.

## Your options before the sale date

A cash sale is only one option, and it isn't always the best one. Before you decide, consider:

- **Talking to your servicer.** Ask about a repayment plan, forbearance or a loan modification. If you want to keep the house and can afford it going forward, this is the place to start.
- **Talking to a HUD-approved housing counselor.** They're free or low-cost and can help you understand every option. You can find one through HUD's website or by calling 800-569-4287.
- **Listing with an agent.** If you have time before the sale date, equity in the home and a house in good condition, a traditional sale may net you more.
- **A short sale or deed in lieu of foreclosure.** If you owe more than the house is worth, your lender may agree to one of these.
- **Talking to an attorney.** A real estate or bankruptcy attorney can explain protections that may apply in your state.

## How a cash sale can stop a foreclosure

When you sell your house, the loan gets paid off at closing, including the past-due payments, late fees and foreclosure costs your servicer is owed. Once the loan is paid off, there's nothing left to foreclose on.

## Watch out for foreclosure rescue scams

Homeowners facing foreclosure are a target for scams. Be cautious of anyone who:

- Asks you to **sign over your deed** with a promise that you can rent the house back and buy it back later
- Wants an **upfront fee** to "save" your home (federal rules generally prohibit mortgage relief companies from charging fees before they deliver results)
- Tells you to **stop paying your servicer** and pay them instead
- Pressures you to sign documents you haven't had time to read or have reviewed

A legitimate buyer will put their offer in writing, close through a licensed title company or closing attorney, and encourage you to get independent advice. We're glad to have your attorney or housing counselor review anything before you sign.
