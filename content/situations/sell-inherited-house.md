Inheriting a house often comes at one of the hardest times in life. On top of grief, you may suddenly be responsible for a property you didn't plan for: a mortgage, property taxes, insurance, repairs, a lifetime of belongings, and maybe family members with different ideas about what to do. You don't have to figure it all out at once.

## First steps after a death in the family

Before anyone decides whether to keep, rent or sell, a few things protect the property and the estate:

- **Find the will** and identify the personal representative (the executor).
- **Secure the house.** Check the locks, stop the mail, and make sure nothing is left running.
- **Call the home insurer.** Policies often change once a home is vacant, especially through an Edmonton winter, so ask what's required to keep coverage.
- **Keep the heat on and the essentials paid**, including the mortgage, property taxes and utilities, so the estate doesn't lose value to penalties or a frozen pipe.
- **Don't give away or sell major items yet.** The estate may need an inventory first.

## Who can sell the house?

Whoever signs the sale needs legal authority to sell. Depending on how the home was owned, that might be:

- **The personal representative**, once the court has issued a grant of probate (when there's a will) or a grant of administration (when there isn't)
- **The surviving joint owner**, if the house was owned in joint tenancy
- **A trustee**, if the house was held in a trust

The estate's lawyer can tell you exactly what's needed for Land Titles, and we're happy to work with them.

## Your options for an inherited home

1. **Keep it** as a home for a family member.
2. **Rent it out.** It produces income, but someone becomes a landlord, and older homes often need work before they're rentable.
3. **List it with a realtor.** Often the best choice when the home is updated and the estate can carry the costs while it sells.
4. **Sell it as-is for cash.** The simplest option when the home needs work, family lives out of town, or everyone wants a clean, quick resolution.

## When family doesn't agree

Disagreements are common, and they usually come down to money and timing. A firm, written cash offer can help by putting a real number on the table that everyone can evaluate. At closing, the money flows through the lawyers to the estate, so no one has to trust anyone else with a cheque.
