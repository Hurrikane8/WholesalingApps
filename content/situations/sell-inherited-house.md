Inheriting a house often comes at one of the hardest times in life. On top of grief, you may suddenly be responsible for a property you didn't plan for: a mortgage, taxes, insurance, repairs, a lifetime of belongings, and maybe relatives with different ideas about what to do. You don't have to figure it all out at once.

## First steps after you inherit a house

Before you decide whether to keep, rent or sell, a few things protect the property and the estate:

- **Find the will and any trust documents,** and identify the executor (sometimes called the personal representative).
- **Secure the house.** Change or check the locks, stop the mail, and make sure nothing is left running.
- **Call the homeowner's insurance company.** Standard policies can limit coverage once a home is vacant, so ask whether you need a vacancy policy.
- **Keep paying the essentials** (mortgage, property taxes, insurance and utilities) so the estate doesn't lose value to penalties or damage.
- **Don't give away or sell major items yet.** The estate may need an inventory first.

## Do you need probate before selling?

Whoever signs the sale paperwork needs the legal authority to sell. Depending on how the property was owned, that might come from:

- **Probate court,** which appoints an executor or administrator who can sell on behalf of the estate
- **A living trust,** where the successor trustee can often sell without going through probate
- **A transfer-on-death deed or joint ownership with right of survivorship,** where the property may pass directly to the new owner

Probate rules and timelines vary by state. A probate attorney can tell you exactly what's needed, and we're happy to work with yours.

## Your options for an inherited house

1. **Keep it** as your home or a vacation property.
2. **Rent it out.** This can produce income, but you'll be a landlord, and older homes often need work before they're rentable.
3. **List it with an agent.** It's often the best choice when the house is updated and the heirs can fund repairs and carrying costs while it sells.
4. **Sell it as-is for cash.** It's the simplest option when the house needs work, the heirs live far away, or everyone just wants a clean, quick resolution.

## When heirs don't agree

Disagreements among siblings and relatives are common, and they usually come down to money and timing. A firm, written cash offer can help by putting a real number on the table that everyone can evaluate. At closing, the title company or closing attorney pays out the proceeds exactly as directed, so no one has to trust anyone else with a check.
