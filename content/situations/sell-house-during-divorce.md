The home is often the biggest asset a couple owns together, and the hardest one to divide. Deciding what to do with it while emotions are running high can drag things out, add legal costs and keep two people tied together long after they're ready to move on.

## Your options for the home

Every situation is different, but most couples choose one of these:

- **One of you keeps it** and buys out the other's share, usually by refinancing into their own name. This works when the person staying qualifies for the mortgage alone.
- **You keep owning it together** for a while, often until the kids finish school. It can work, but it keeps you financially linked.
- **You list it with a realtor.** Often the highest price for a well-kept home, but it means agreeing on repairs, price, offers and conditions, and months of showings.
- **You sell it as-is for cash.** One number, one closing and a clean split.

## Why a cash sale can make separation easier

- **Fewer decisions to agree on.** No debating repairs, list price, staging or counteroffers.
- **No showings.** Nobody has to keep the house spotless or coordinate schedules with an ex.
- **Certainty.** No buyer financing to fall through halfway to closing.
- **A neutral, written number.** A clear offer is something both lawyers can review and both of you can evaluate on its own merits.
- **Speed, or not.** Close in as little as {{closeDays}} days, or set a date that lines up with your agreement.

## What you'll need

- **Signatures from everyone on title**, plus any spousal consent your lawyer says is required, or a court order authorizing the sale
- **Agreement (or a court order) on how the proceeds will be divided**
- **Independent legal advice**, which we always encourage before anyone signs

At closing, the lawyers pay out the mortgage and anything else on title, then handle the rest as directed.
