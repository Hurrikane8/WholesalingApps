The house is often the biggest asset a couple owns together, and the hardest one to divide. Deciding what to do with it while emotions are running high can drag out a divorce, add expense and keep two people tied together long after they're ready to move on.

## Your options for the house

Every situation is different, but most couples choose one of these:

- **One spouse keeps the house** and buys out the other's share, usually by refinancing into their own name. This works when the remaining spouse qualifies for the loan alone.
- **You keep owning it together** for a period of time, often until the kids finish school. This can work, but it keeps you financially linked.
- **You list it with an agent.** It often brings the highest price for a well-kept home, but it means repairs, showings and months of agreeing on price, offers and repair requests.
- **You sell it as-is for cash.** You get one number, one closing and a clean split.

## Why a cash sale can make divorce easier

- **Fewer decisions to agree on.** No debating repairs, list price, staging, open houses or counteroffers.
- **No showings.** Nobody has to keep the house spotless or coordinate schedules with an ex.
- **Certainty.** No buyer financing to fall through halfway to closing.
- **A neutral, written number.** A clear offer is something both attorneys can review and both spouses can evaluate on its own merits.
- **Speed, or not.** Close in as little as {{closeDays}} days, or set a date that lines up with your settlement.

## What you'll need

- **Signatures from everyone on the title,** or a court order authorizing the sale
- **Agreement (or a court order) on how the proceeds will be split**
- **Your attorneys' review,** which we always encourage before anyone signs

At closing, the title company or closing attorney pays off the mortgage and any liens, then disburses what's left to each of you as directed.

## A note on timing

In some states, filing for divorce automatically restricts either spouse from selling or transferring marital property without the other's consent or a court order. Before you sign anything, check with your attorney about what's allowed at your stage of the process.
