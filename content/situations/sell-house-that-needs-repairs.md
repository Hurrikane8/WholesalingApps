Some houses need a little paint. Others need a new roof, a foundation repair, rewiring or a complete renovation. When the list gets long, a traditional sale gets hard: lenders and insurers get nervous about homes in rough shape, and buyers who do make an offer ask for repairs or big price cuts after the inspection.

## Why repairs make a home hard to sell

- **Financing and insurance.** Lenders may not finance a home with serious problems, and some insurers won't cover homes with issues like Poly-B plumbing or knob-and-tube wiring until they're replaced.
- **Inspection conditions.** Even after you accept an offer, the buyer's inspection can reopen the price or end the deal.
- **Upfront cash.** Repairs have to be paid for before you sell, and good contractors are often booked weeks or months out.
- **Uncertain returns.** Many renovations don't add as much value as they cost, especially at retail labour and material prices.

## Repair first or sell as-is?

Repairing first can make sense when the work is mostly cosmetic, you have the cash and the time, and updated homes in your area sell at a real premium. If that's your situation, I'll tell you. Selling as-is makes sense when:

- the repairs are major (structural, roof, mould, electrical, plumbing, furnace or boiler);
- you don't have the cash, or don't want to borrow it;
- you don't have months to manage contractors;
- you'd rather have certainty now than a possibly higher price later.

## Problems that don't stop a sale

- Foundation cracks, settling, and basement water or sewer backup
- Roof leaks, ice damming and hail damage
- Mould and old water damage
- Poly-B or Kitec plumbing, and knob-and-tube or aluminum wiring
- Vermiculite insulation in the attic
- Failed furnaces, boilers and hot water tanks
- Fire damage (see [selling a fire-damaged house](/situations/sell-fire-damaged-house))
- Hoarding situations and homes full of belongings
- Unpermitted basement suites and additions

## How I price a home that needs work

I estimate what the home would likely sell for once it's fully repaired, based on recent comparable sales, then subtract my estimate of the repairs, the costs to buy, hold and resell it, and a profit. Your written offer lays out each number, so you can check my work. More on [how cash offers are calculated](/blog/how-cash-home-buyers-calculate-offers).

## Honesty goes both ways

Selling as-is means you won't make repairs. It doesn't mean hiding known problems. Tell me what you know. It won't put me off, and it keeps the deal clean for everyone.
