Some houses need a little paint. Others need a new roof, a foundation repair, rewiring or a total renovation. When the list gets long, a traditional sale gets hard: many buyers can't finance a house in rough shape, and the ones who can will ask for repairs or big price cuts after the inspection.

## Why repairs make a house hard to sell

- **Financing rules.** Many mortgages, especially FHA and VA loans, require the home to meet minimum condition standards. Homes with safety or structural issues may not qualify.
- **Inspection negotiations.** Even after you accept an offer, the buyer's inspection can reopen the price and the repair list.
- **Upfront cash.** Repairs have to be paid for before you sell, and contractors are often booked weeks or months out.
- **Uncertain returns.** Many renovations don't add as much value as they cost, especially when you're paying retail for labor and materials.

## Repair first or sell as-is?

Repairing first can make sense when the work is mostly cosmetic, you have the cash and time, and the local market strongly rewards updated homes. Selling as-is makes sense when:

- The repairs are major (structural, roof, mold, electrical, plumbing, HVAC)
- You don't have the cash, or don't want to borrow it
- You don't have months to manage contractors
- You'd rather have certainty now than a possibly higher price later

## Problems we regularly buy through

- Foundation cracks, settling and water intrusion
- Roof leaks and storm damage
- Mold, moisture and old water damage
- Outdated or unsafe electrical and plumbing
- Failed HVAC, water heaters and septic systems
- Termite and pest damage
- Fire damage (see [selling a fire-damaged house](/situations/sell-fire-damaged-house))
- Hoarding situations and houses full of belongings
- Code violations and unpermitted additions

## Honesty goes both ways

Selling as-is means you won't make repairs. It generally doesn't mean you can hide known problems. Many states require sellers to disclose known material defects even in an as-is sale. Tell us what you know. It won't scare us away, and it keeps the deal clean.
