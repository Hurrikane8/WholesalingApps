Being a landlord looked good on paper. Then came the late rent, the 11 p.m. plumbing call, the turnover repairs and the tenant who stopped answering the phone. If you're ready to be done, you don't have to wait for the lease to end, or for the tenant to leave, to sell.

## Signs it may be time to sell

- Rent comes in late (or not at all) more often than it comes in on time
- Repairs and turnovers are eating the cash flow
- The property needs big-ticket items (a roof, HVAC, a sewer line) you don't want to fund
- You're dealing with an eviction, code enforcement or an unresponsive property manager
- You'd rather have the equity working somewhere else, or not working at all

## Your options as a landlord

- **Hire a property manager.** This hands off the day-to-day, but typically costs a percentage of the rent plus leasing fees, and the repairs are still yours.
- **Wait for the lease to end and list it vacant.** You'll likely need turnover repairs and months of carrying costs with no rent coming in.
- **Sell it occupied to an investor.** This is exactly what we do.

## Selling with tenants in place

When you sell an occupied rental, the lease generally stays in effect and transfers to the new owner. Here's how we handle it:

- **Walkthrough on the tenants' schedule,** with proper notice as your lease and state law require
- **Lease and deposit transfer at closing,** handled by the title company or closing attorney
- **We take it from there.** After closing, the tenants deal with us, not you

## What if the tenant isn't paying?

We buy properties with non-paying tenants and even with evictions in progress. The important thing is to follow the legal process in your state; "self-help" steps like changing the locks or shutting off utilities can create legal liability for landlords. Once we close, the situation becomes ours to resolve.
