After a fire, a burst pipe, a flood or a storm, the damage to the house is only part of the problem. There's the insurance claim, the adjuster visits, the contractor bids, the smell, the mold risk and the question of where you're going to live while all of it gets sorted out. For many owners, rebuilding just isn't worth it.

## First things first after a fire or water damage

- **Make sure everyone is safe,** and don't enter a damaged structure until the fire department or a professional says it's safe.
- **Call your insurance company** and start the claim as soon as possible.
- **Document everything** with photos and video before anything is moved or removed.
- **Prevent further damage** where you can: board up openings, tarp the roof and stop the water. Many policies expect this.
- **Get a copy of the fire report** if there was a fire; your insurer will likely ask for it.

## Rebuild or sell?

Rebuilding can make sense when the damage is limited, insurance will cover most of the cost, and you want to stay in the home. Selling as-is often makes more sense when:

- The repair estimate is close to, or more than, the insurance payout
- Contractors are booked out for months
- You've already found somewhere else to live
- The house was a rental or an inherited property you didn't plan to keep
- You simply don't want to manage a major rebuild

## How insurance fits in

Every policy is different, so talk with your adjuster before deciding. In general:

- Your claim is based on your policy and the damage, so selling the house doesn't automatically end your claim, but ask your adjuster how a sale affects it.
- If you have a mortgage, insurance checks for structural damage are often made payable to you **and** your lender.
- At closing, the mortgage is paid off from the sale price, which can simplify how remaining insurance funds are released.
